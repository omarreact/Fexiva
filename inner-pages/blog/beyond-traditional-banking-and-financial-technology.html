Beyond Traditional Banking And Financial Technology

Sophie Turner
7 Min

Financial Technology Did Not Kill The Bank. It Broke Banking Into Parts And Let Businesses Choose Only The Pieces They Need.

The first wave of fintech focused on consumers: faster transfers, cleaner apps and fewer fees. The second wave is aimed squarely at companies, and it treats payments, cards, lending and reporting as building blocks rather than a single package sold by a single institution on a single set of terms.

Why Unbundling Matters For Growing Teams

A five-person startup and a five-hundred-person company do not need the same financial stack, yet traditional banking sold both of them the same account. Unbundling lets a team start with the basics, add capability as the business grows and avoid migrating to a new provider every eighteen months.

The Building Blocks

  • Instant issuing for physical and virtual cards, with limits and merchant rules set per card rather than per account.
  • Embedded lending that prices credit on live revenue data instead of a two-year-old balance sheet, so a company with strong recent growth is judged on what it is doing now and not on the year it was still finding its footing.
  • Open APIs that keep accounting, payroll and procurement tools in sync without a nightly export or a person copying numbers between tabs.

How To Evaluate A Provider

  1. Ask how data leaves the platform, not just how it arrives. A tool that imports beautifully but exports a monthly PDF will become the bottleneck the moment you add a second system.
  2. Run the approval flow with a real purchase and count how many people it touches, how long each step waits and whether the final record would satisfy an auditor without any explanation.

What Traditional Banks Still Do Well

Deposit protection, regulatory depth and long-term credit relationships remain the domain of established institutions, and that is unlikely to change soon.

The smartest fintech products partner with banks rather than pretending they do not exist, sitting on top of regulated accounts and adding the software layer that banks have historically struggled to build. For a business, the practical answer is not to choose between the two but to combine a trusted balance sheet with the tools that make the money on it easier to move, control and understand.

How long does it take to get started?

Most teams are fully onboarded in under a day. Card issuing, ERP sync and approval workflows are configured from a guided two-minute setup, no engineering work required.

Do I need to connect my bank or ERP to use Fexiva?

No. You can start with a Fexiva account and cards on day one. Bank and ERP connections are optional and can be added at any time from Settings.

Can I cancel or change my plan at any time?

Yes. Upgrade, downgrade or cancel from your billing page whenever you like. Changes take effect at the start of the next billing cycle.

How does Fexiva keep our financial data secure?

All data is encrypted in transit and at rest, access is protected with two-factor authentication and every action is logged in a tamper-proof audit trail.

Do you support companies operating in multiple currencies?

Yes. Hold balances in 75+ currencies, pay suppliers locally and see consolidated reporting in your home currency.

Which accounting tools does Fexiva integrate with?

Fexiva syncs natively with popular accounting, ERP and payroll tools, and connects to 100+ other apps through its open API.

Can I import existing cards and expenses?

Yes. Upload a CSV or connect your old provider and we will migrate card holders, limits and historical transactions for you.

Is there an onboarding team to help us roll out?

Enterprise and Business plans include a dedicated onboarding specialist who configures approval workflows and trains your team.

Is Fexiva PCI DSS and SOC 2 compliant?

Yes. Fexiva is PCI DSS Level 1 certified and audited annually for SOC 2 Type II. Reports are available on request.

Where is our data stored?

Data is stored in regional data centres in the EU and US. You choose the region when you create your workspace.

Can we control who sees what inside the company?

Role-based permissions let you decide who can issue cards, approve spend and view reports, down to the team or cost centre.
